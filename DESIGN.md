# Implemented rules and design decisions

Native sandbox status: infantry bayonet charges, all twelve commander-owned
acquired abilities, Python-compatible custom recipes, terrain generation,
transactional manual deployment, and frozen results have joined the C# engine.
The ability registry reuses Python's parameters with category/role eligibility,
a three-ability setup limit, bounded stacking and fresh per-battle activation
state. Abilities are assigned freely in setup; campaign rewards and persistence
remain Python-only. [SANDBOX.md](godot/SANDBOX.md) specifies the current native
rules, controls and remaining limits.


The Python design below remains the reference. The native Godot/C# engine now
implements movement, terrain, combat, morale, cavalry, physical artillery and all
six specialist archetypes, autonomous tactical AI and named commanders with starting
personalities/specializations. See [AI and commander rules](godot/AI_COMMANDERS.md) and [Milestone 4 rules](godot/ARTILLERY_SPECIALISTS.md)
for authoritative native scope, port fidelity and deferred AI/commander features.
Starting commander traits are active; acquired upgrades and campaign remain deferred in C#.

## Simulation and commands

The field is 1,000 × 600 metres with a 0.1 s fixed tick. The renderer uses an accumulator independent of simulation speed. Simulation work has an 8 ms/four-tick per-frame budget. All elapsed simulation demand is retained under overload, with pending simulation seconds reported in the HUD; only visual camera panning caps elapsed contribution at 0.25 s. Tick IDs remain consecutive and timestep unchanged. Render interpolation smooths movement between ticks; see the performance section below.

`UnitStats` contains immutable type characteristics; `Regiment` holds mutable battle state. `Command` and `Order` are the seam for future command systems. Hold, move, advance, attack, defend, reserve, skirmish and withdraw exist in the simulation; defend is currently available through the Python command API rather than a GUI shortcut. Reserve stays put and fires autonomously. Withdraw heads toward the army's home edge and removes the regiment on arrival. Routed units ignore ordinary commands; rallying units remain stationary. There are no soldiers, ECS, external physics, or campaign abstractions.

## Movement and formation

Infantry line is 64 m wide × 12 m deep, column 22 × 34 m. Line uses 75% movement and 65% turning rates; column uses full rates and 32% firing frontage. Changes take seven seconds, stop movement/fire, and cost six cohesion. The old footprint remains until the change completes. Cavalry and artillery use fixed 40 × 18 and 28 × 18 m footprints respectively.

Base speeds: infantry 2.8, cavalry 7.5, artillery 1.8 m/s. Base turn rates: 0.65, 1.1, 0.4 rad/s, also affected by formation. Troops slow while facing away from their destination; fatigue and morale reduce movement. Sharp turns cost cohesion. Corners are clamped to the field.

### Friendly soft collisions and congestion

Friendly rectangles remain passable and receive no instantaneous collision push. Persistent positioning conflicts use gradual normal movement, described below. Exact oriented rectangle intersection area is calculated with convex polygon clipping. For each regiment, overlap severity is intersection area divided by its own formation area, taking the **maximum** over friends. Edge touching gives zero severity. This intentionally ignores the union of several small intersections: a bounded worst-pair approximation prevents penalties stacking excessively in dense crowds.

`simulation/formations.py` defines default `CongestionProfile` values. They can be overridden per battle through `Rules.congestion_profiles`:

| Formation | Maximum speed reduction | Cohesion loss at full overlap, points/s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Line | 45% | 2.4 |
| Column | 25% | 0.9 |
| Skirmish | 30% | 1.1 |

The speed multiplier is `max(congestion_min_speed, 1 - reduction * max(overlap_fraction * directional_cost))`, with a floor of 0.5 in `simulation/config.py`. This floor applies to congestion alone: facing, fatigue, morale, terrain and formation changes retain their effects. Direction uses measured post-move velocity, including boundary clamping, rather than facing. For two speeds above 0.1 m/s, let `same=(1+cos(angle))/2`. Directional cost is `clamp(1 - same*(1-low) + 0.35*same*abs(speed_a-speed_b)/max(speed_a,speed_b), 0, 1)`. The aligned low cost is 0.20 for ordinary pairs, 0.06 for two cavalry, or 0.02 for two cavalry pursuing the same routed target with cosine above 0.8. Thus opposite directions retain full cost; perpendicular movement is intermediate, and overtaking costs more than equal-speed travel. Shared targets never exempt opposing motion. Movement through stationary troops has full cost. These values are Rules parameters.

Cohesion disruption uses the same bounded worst-pair directional cost, applied once after movement in simulation seconds. Stationary troops take 35% of a moving troop's disruption. Two stationary overlapping formations have no continuous congestion cohesion loss or direct morale shock. There is no added recovery system. Compatible cavalry pursuit also uses stable lateral lanes, centred by unit-ID order at 16 m spacing (capped at 24 m or 30% target frontage). Offsets taper to zero near contact, do not change targets, and leave contact/charge resolution authoritative.

Destinations and detours are computed from shared pre-movement positions. Pre-move overlap controls the tick's speed; post-move overlap controls that tick's cohesion loss and debug state. The speed modifier can therefore change one tick before its displayed speed reflects it. Recovery uses the existing morale module (also called in the drill sandbox): 0.65 cohesion/s resting or 0.1 moving, only while idle and free of active crossing disruption. Two stationary overlapping units can redress and recover, so a rallying unit cannot be trapped forever by a stationary friend. The existing rally recovery bonus is likewise disabled during active crossings.

### Limited avoidance and fallback

Move, advance and defend orders may try **one detour per destination** around a stationary friendly obstruction within 150 m. Both lateral candidates are evaluated; a conservative swept-circle envelope bounds the turning formation, and both legs must clear every other regiment and remain within the field. The detour is accepted only if it adds at most 100 m and 50% distance. The shorter candidate wins, with deterministic right-hand tie-breaking. This is deliberately conservative and may pass through friends even where a more elaborate route could avoid them.

Moving friends are crossed directly, preventing mutual avoidance dances. An accepted waypoint persists until reached, congestion interrupts it, or 120 seconds expire. After that, movement continues directly without another detour for that destination. A changed order resets the budget; a destination shift over 20 m also resets it for dynamic orders. Routing and withdrawing skip detours so friendly troops cannot obstruct their escape. No formation is changed automatically.

Enemy pairs retain the existing contact/melee checks and three bounded SAT separation passes; only friendly pairs are excluded from separation. No combat damage or engagement rule was changed. Small enemy penetration during a tick is still intentional so existing contact triggers work before separation. This is a local contact solver, not a general rigid-body system: dense multi-enemy contact and enemy encirclement can still block movement. Very high custom speeds beyond the supported baselines would need swept regiment collision detection. Overlapping symbols may obscure selection or debug labels; selecting a mover before a crossing or zooming in helps, and selection cycling remains unimplemented. Formation changes remain instantaneous footprint changes after their timer.

Tunables are `Rules.congestion_*`, `Rules.cohesion_*` and `Rules.avoidance_*` in `simulation/config.py`, plus the three default congestion profiles in `simulation/formations.py`. Soft passage makes friendly traffic reliable; it does not solve unreachable goals outside the field, enemy encirclement, or globally optimal routing.

### Persistent overlap and accepted stopping positions

`simulation/positioning.py` reuses the pre-movement overlap pairs. Both regiments must be effective, not changing formation or in melee, and holding/reserving/defending, at the end of a Move, or stopped under Advance. Attack, skirmish, withdrawal, rout and rally are excluded. Substantial overlap means at least 25% of **each** footprint for two simulated seconds; a near-arrival Move within 12 m also qualifies. Merely touching or crossing en route does not trigger planning.

The higher priority regiment keeps its position: explicit player Hold, other Hold/Reserve, firing, then ordinary positioning, with longer rest time and lower stable ID breaking ties. The loser tries up to 24 nearby endpoint candidates within 110 m of its preferred destination. Candidates use the intended final oriented rectangle with 4 m clearance, full turning-circle boundary clearance, current/reserved friendly footprints, enemy footprints and conservative enemy swept-route rejection. Forest/slope cost is a small tie preference; no terrain becomes impassable. A spatial index is built only when planning is actually needed. A failed plan retries after five seconds; an unfinished movement times out after 60 seconds. There is no global packing/pathfinding pass.

The accepted goal overrides movement intent while preserving the original Order and preferred location. Travel and final facing use existing speed/turning constraints, and final goals remain stable after arrival until the order/context changes. Pending goals reserve their final footprint. No teleportation, opposing pushes or repeated per-tick candidate searches occur. A new player order cancels the accepted goal. F3 shows overlap duration, accepted goal, preferred position and last-volley frontage; a subtle purple marker also indicates an adjusted stopping position in normal play.

Dense crowds, turns during later combat, enemy encirclement, limited search directions or boundaries can leave residual overlap when no candidate fits. Such overlap remains passable and its musket frontage is restricted immediately, even before separation succeeds. Separation does not proactively adjust arbitrary moving attack destinations or displace melee participants. It cannot guarantee a perfect battle line under every arrangement.

## Infantry and melee

Infantry range is 155 m, reload 12 s, half arc 0.65 rad. Flat-terrain line of sight is blocked by separate intervening formations, with endpoint overlap groups exempt as described below. Statistical volleys sample independent Bernoulli hits from 42% of remaining strength, scaled by formation. Base accuracy is 14%, declining with range; cohesion, moving fire and shaken/wavering states affect effectiveness. These are compressed game values, not historical reconstructions. Volleys reduce morale even with no kills, representing sustained fire.

Organized contact resolves every two seconds per pair. Losses scale with strength, cohesion, morale-state effectiveness and seeded variation. Ordinary melee flank and rear multipliers are 1.6 and 2.2. Cavalry impact is now a separate one-time event built from actual movement; pursuit has its own interval budget, described below. Stationary, steady, cohesive infantry strongly resists a frontal charge. Artillery is weak in melee. Individual horses/bayonets and square formation remain omitted. Nearby enemies cannot all be optimally targeted; imperfect order execution is intentional.

## Morale and cohesion

Both are independently bounded to 0–100. Casualties, volleys, contact, outnumbering and nearby routs reduce morale. Sharp turning, changing formation and damage reduce cohesion. Quiet troops recover cohesion; safe troops recover morale and fatigue. Successful attacks and nearby enemy routs give small morale benefits. Discipline is a simple scalar on shock/recovery, leaving room for future unit traits.

Steady becomes shaken below 60, wavering below 40, routing at 23. Recovery requires 66 and 46 respectively, giving hysteresis. Rout events emit one distance-scaled shock to effective friends within 145 m. Consequent routs happen on later ticks; no recursive propagation or repeated shock from an ongoing rout. Metrics count rout transitions and nearby rout shocks, not unique regiments or proven causal chains.

Routed units steer away from nearby threats, retain their escape direction once clear and may withdraw at any edge. With no established danger/escape direction they initially head home. After 22 seconds without an enemy inside 160 m, survivors above 15% initial strength begin rallying. At 48 morale and 45 cohesion they return shaken to their previous order. New threats abort rallying. This permits recurring rallies, and can produce long tails in a battle. An army loses when its effective non-routing/non-rallying strength remains below 18% of its initial strength for 12 seconds. Simultaneous collapse is a draw. The sandbox disables combat and victory.

## Artillery

Field battery range is 440 m, reload 19 s, with six guns at full crew strength (horse batteries: 360 m, 17 s, four lighter guns). Salvo size scales with surviving crew. Each ball launches at 100 m/s toward an independent seeded aim point with up to ±45 m error per axis, with vertical speed chosen to land near that point. Point-blank scatter is capped at half the muzzle-to-target distance to prevent backwards shots; forest and commander modifiers still apply. Gravity is 9.81 m/s². Ground impacts retain 48% vertical speed, 78% horizontal speed and 72% damage energy. At most five bounces or 14 seconds lifetime. Low passages through footprints (up to 3 m high) cause losses and shock. Swept segment/rectangle intersection prevents tunnelling, sorted by passage time. A ball can hit multiple regiments including friends. Each regiment is hit at most once over a ball's entire lifetime, a deliberately stricter rule than once per continuous passage.

Projectiles may overshoot through penetration and bouncing. Artillery launch eligibility ignores line of sight through both friendly and enemy formations. Range, firing arc, reload and formation-change restrictions still apply. Ballistic height, swept collisions, bouncing and energy loss determine the actual victims, including friendly fire; no safe-trajectory prediction is performed. The vertical motion is simplified per tick, with no material-specific penetration or individual tree collisions; nonempty terrain uses substeps and ground-contact bisection as described below. Smoke is a visual event, with no visibility effect.

## AI and feedback

Default player troops retain scenario orders; the opposing controller replans every five seconds. Infantry forms the centre and stops at 85% musket range; one regiment waits as reserve until 75 s or a nearby threat. Artillery holds its initial rear position and can fire without a clear direct lane. Cavalry tries a field-edge approach against exposed/weak targets. It has no global formation solver, strategic retreat or graphical fog of war; local artillery position searches and specialist behaviour are described below. Its flank approach can be inefficient, and it can waste cavalry. Both sides can use this controller in spectator mode.

The bounded event log explains significant losses, charges, morale shocks, engagement, formation changes, routs, rallies, friendly fire and victory. Cosmetic smoke and impacts are time-stamped simulation effects and do not consume extra randomness in rendering. Firing snapshots the muzzle position, facing and frontage; filled translucent plumes expand/drift/fade for three simulated seconds, with a 0.22-second flash. This replaces the old hard-to-see outline ring. Debug views expose targeting, active cyan detours, and selected congestion percentage, speed multiplier, actual speed and AI intent. Congested formations have an orange outline even with debugging off. No sound, unit sprite assets, live-battle saves, minimap, campaign, active ability controls, experience or generic modifiers framework is included.

## Validation and balance priorities

Automated tests cover deterministic command replay; motion and turns; camera transforms; SAT rectangles; line of sight, range and arcs; casualty bounds and formation firepower; frontal versus rear charges; morale hysteresis, rout propagation and rallying; ballistic sweeps and friendly fire; victory and full headless battle completion; GUI command translation and read-only rendering with a dummy display.

The initial prototype's 18-test suite passed on Python 3.14.7 / Pygame CE 2.5.8 on Linux. Its baseline batch seeds 1–3 completed at 524.1, 481.5 and 789.9 seconds: two Albion wins and one Continental win, with one friendly-fire incident. These pre-soft-collision baseline results remain in `analysis/sample_results.json`; they are not results for the current movement rules. The third battle exceeds the intended ten-minute upper target. This is evidence that the interactions work, not evidence of balance. The standard scenario needs broader seed sampling, alternative deployments and human tactical playtesting. Both graphical scenarios were smoke-tested with SDL dummy drivers; actual desktop interaction, Python 3.11 and Windows have not been exercised.

With the revised movement rules, seed 1 completes at 1,059 simulated seconds (17 routs, 14 rallies, two friendly-fire events), rather than its old 524-second baseline. It continues fighting and rallying rather than deadlocking. The headless completion test now permits 1,200 seconds instead of asserting the previous 650-second balance window. Combat and victory parameters were deliberately not retuned in this movement task; the original 5–10 minute battle target needs another balance pass.

All 35 tests pass after the movement revision, including headless completion and every scripted demonstration order. The movement revision adds regression coverage for stationary crossings, opposing friendly movers, dense screens, short detours, timeout fallback, configurable line/column penalties, oriented overlap area, directional/time-based cohesion costs, enemy melee contact, deterministic tick scheduling, retreat through allies and smoke rendering. GUI checks use SDL's dummy driver and screenshot inspection, not live desktop interaction.

Next priorities: tune congestion costs with human playtesting and improve dense enemy contact; reduce battle-ending rally loops; improve battery positioning; improve cavalry approach and reserve triggers; tune morale and losses across many seeds; add a better event inspection panel. Tune primary values in `simulation/config.py`, unit baselines in `regiment.py`, and formation dimensions in `formations.py`; some local formula coefficients remain in combat/morale modules for readability. The desired degree of direct control, historical roster and campaign design remain open future decisions; none blocks this prototype.

## Custom battle recipes and deployment

`scenarios/battle_config.py` separates frozen starting data from live battle state. `BattleConfig.prepared()` resolves a missing seed and produces/validates placements; retain this returned value to restart exactly. `create_battle()` constructs fresh regiments, RNG, timers, projectiles, events and command queues. Saving writes only the recipe. JSON version 1 uses existing unit characteristics and simulation rules; weapon tuning is outside this format. Presets use the same schema and are packaged with the game.

Automatic deployment uses finite 56 × 90 m grid slots within zones: blue from x=20 m to 32% field width, red from 68% width to width−20 m, both inset 20 m vertically. The neutral gap is 36% field width. Units face the opposition. Infantry prefers front centre slots, cavalry front flank slots, artillery one rank behind infantry with a preference for clear rows. Extra troops use more depth. Grid spacing bounds all initial line/cavalry/battery footprints; exact SAT checks validate the resulting arrangement. Small/Medium/Large have capacities of 12/30/90 automatic slots per army, subject to the lower default 48-regiment combined limit. This conservative packer may reject an army that a tighter hand-designed layout could fit; it never silently overlaps units or performs unbounded searches.

Deployment edits are transactional. Candidate positions, rotations, formations and orders are validated together against full footprints, zone boundaries and all other regiments. Rejected edits display a red ghost while leaving the last valid recipe intact. Selected reset can be rejected if its automatic location is occupied; Reset all is always a clean automatic layout. For a valid imported deployment denser than the conservative grid, resets use the imported starting layout instead. Blue can be dragged in the GUI; both armies can be edited through the data model. Frozen deployment previews render ordinary battle objects but never step them or accept combat commands.

`viewer/application.py` owns the setup/deployment/battle phases, fixed-step accumulator and immutable starting recipe. Returning to setup preserves the last recipe, not damaged live regiments. F5 rebuilds from that recipe, F6 changes only its seed, and F7 edits it. Changing army counts or dimensions regenerates placement, while seed-only edits retain it. AI-controlled armies still replan from tick zero; disable their AI in JSON/API to keep initial orders indefinitely.

Map rendering/grid bounds, move-click clamps, initial camera fit and cavalry flank waypoints now use configured dimensions. Reserve selection uses the last infantry regiment when at least four remain, rather than an ID modulo six. Batteries without infantry support can advance into weapon range, making artillery-only setups functional on large maps. The legacy 1,000 × 600 m scenarios remain available explicitly; no movement, combat damage or morale rules were rewritten for setup.

Validation covers composition/category/combined caps, finite sizes and coordinates, unique IDs, matching rosters, supported formations/orders, valid enemy targets, full deployment footprints, schema version, malformed JSON and file-size limits. Separate tests exercise frozen preview input, manual deployment rollback, save/load, menu scaling, identical restart/rematch, fresh runtime state, headless config batches and atypical AI rosters. Menus are tested at 960 × 640, 1280 × 720 and 1920 × 1080 through SDL's dummy driver. Desktop interaction and Windows remain unverified.

Remaining limitations: deployment packing is intentionally conservative; many units reduce simulation speed; unusual rosters can be unbalanced or stall tactically; initial orders for an AI army can be overwritten immediately; the GUI edits only blue placement; file paths are text fields rather than a file chooser. Setup itself introduces no scenario scripting, army budget or campaign system; hills and forests are described below.


## Selected target feedback

Selected regiments show a red enemy reticle/footprint outline and dashed connector independently of the F3 overlay. A separate cyan diamond identifies an explicit ordered enemy when it differs from the current target. Read-only `viewer/targeting.py` shares validated target lookup and firing status between renderer and HUD; rendering does not advance simulation or RNG. The status distinguishes range, arc, line-of-sight, reload and formation-change constraints. Current targets are aiming/pursuit intent, not a guarantee of firing or hitting. Melee can affect multiple contacting enemies, and physical projectiles can strike other units.

Inspection found that ranged selection previously overwrote an explicit attack target with the nearest shootable enemy even when the designated target was shootable. The ranged pass now treats a live explicit Attack target as binding, including while it is not shootable; it falls back to automatic selection only when the designated enemy is no longer active. Other standing orders retain opportunistic fire. Ordinary automatic targeting is unchanged. The order-execution pass clears old targets before early routing/rallying/withdrawal returns, preventing stale pursuit targets; withdrawing troops can still acquire a valid firing target through existing automatic fire rules. Tests cover priority, fallback, firing constraints, invalid/stale target filtering, and visible selected markers without debug mode or state mutation.


### Attack-order execution fixes

Two reproducible failures remained after adding target markers: ranged attackers stopped at their preferred firing distance even with a friendly screen blocking the shot, and the five-second army AI update could replace a player's attack before a slow formation finished turning. Explicit in-range infantry attacks turn in place when the lane is clear, or continue forward into a friendly screen using soft collisions until the shot is eligible. Joining the screen's overlap group now permits firing as described below. Artillery ignores direct-fire screens and holds position to aim instead. A separate intervening enemy still blocks infantry fire and movement normally; there is no new flanking solver or friendly-fire exception for muskets.

A runtime `player_attack` flag protects live player-issued, targeted Attack commands from army replanning while their enemy remains active. A different player order clears the flag; army AI resumes normal planning after the target is gone. Initial orders loaded from configuration remain AI-managed according to `ai_armies`. This flag is fresh on restart and is not serialized as campaign or mid-battle state.

`combat.attack_target`, `select_fire_target` and `firing_blocker` share valid-target, focus and obstruction rules with order execution and HUD diagnostics. Explicit attacks no longer switch their volleys to a different enemy while the ordered target is temporarily blocked, outside the arc or out of range. Reloads and formation transitions are preserved, and named blockers explain why an in-range target is not being shot. Regression tests cover screen clearance (including initial friendly overlap), turning toward a rear target beyond an AI replan, target death/order replacement releasing AI protection, enemy screens, and reload preservation on target switches.

### Firing during soft overlaps

Coincident friendly rectangles previously screened each other in both directions, making units unable to shoot or be shot. `combat.firing_groups()` retains connected positive-area friendly overlap components for target vulnerability: the target's group cannot shield it. Source exemptions apply only to **direct** overlap, so a transitive chain cannot grant unrestricted fire through a separate forward rank. The geometry cache invalidates after pose, formation, withdrawal or strength/active changes as before.

Ready infantry volleys additionally call `friendly_fire_factor()`. Dense direct overlaps share frontage using `1 / (1 + sum(overlap_area / shooter_area * density))`. Three identical dense lines therefore each fire roughly one third of their usual volley and suppression shock, rather than three full volleys or no volleys. Separate friendly screens use three parallel rays at -35%, centre and +35% of the shooter's frontage, with the worst screen's coverage reducing the remaining frontage. Dense full-width screens block fire, narrow screens leave partial lanes, and skirmish density is 0.35. Samples can clear a lower screen when the interpolated sight-line height exceeds its centre ground plus observer height by 0.5 m. Terrain LOS remains independently authoritative. These are approximate lanes, not individual ballistics or exact terrain heights across a whole formation.

Checks use the existing fixed-phase firing spatial index, expanded by shooter half-width, and detailed attenuation runs only for a ready actual volley. Clear flanks return full frontage. Partial crossings temporarily attenuate volleys and stop doing so after clearance. Existing congestion cohesion costs are not reapplied here. The volley still damages its designated target statistically; casualties are not distributed among overlapping enemies. Artillery bypasses this abstract frontage system and retains physical collisions, friendly fire and elevation physics. This deliberately changes volley strength/RNG progression and therefore historical seed outcomes; current-version fixed-seed replay remains deterministic.


### Artillery without line of sight

`combat.firing_blocker()` returns no eligibility blocker for artillery, so firing, target selection, attack positioning and HUD status share the same rule. Infantry still uses the original segment/rectangle obstruction check. This does not remove physical collisions: launched cannonballs retain their existing height, gravity, bounce, penetration and friendly-fire rules. Tests cover friendly and enemy screens, stationary firing through a screen, ordered-target retention, range/arc/reload restrictions, infantry LOS preservation and a launched ball striking an intervening friendly regiment.


## Terrain representation and reproducibility

`simulation/terrain.py` is independent of Pygame. Frozen `TerrainMap` values contain `Hill` features and elliptical `Forest` regions. Legacy Gaussian elevation is the sum `height * exp(-0.5 * ((dx/rx)^2 + (dy/ry)^2))`; negative amplitudes create valleys. Analytic gradients give rise/run slopes. Forest density is the maximum overlapping region density with a smoothstep taper across the outer 20% of each ellipse. Arbitrary world-coordinate queries are continuous; map edges do not introduce height discontinuities.

Six presets are available in setup and via CLI `--terrain`: Open Field, Central Hill, Rolling Hills, Wooded Battlefield, Mixed Terrain and Ridge Defence. Feature counts grow with field area, while radii and height distributions remain fixed in metres; see the update below. Central Hill and Ridge Defence are fixed; hills/forests in the other applicable presets use a local terrain RNG. Neither generation nor cosmetic trees consume combat randomness. Terrain features are immutable and serialized explicitly in the existing optional version-1 configuration extension. Old files default to Open Field. Preparation resolves a separate terrain seed once; restart and new-combat-seed rematch preserve geometry and deployment. Regenerate map deliberately resets placement. Valid imported custom geometry remains editable and is not silently replaced by an automatic map.

### Movement, formation and cohesion

Terrain is sampled at the regiment centre and four footprint corners. The signed mean gradient along movement determines uphill/downhill speed; mean gradient magnitude determines difficult-slope disruption. The forest density average controls vegetation effects. The approximate footprint may straddle different terrain without deforming the rectangle.

| Type | Full forest slowdown | Forest cohesion loss per metre | Uphill sensitivity | Steep cohesion factor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Infantry | 0.35 | 0.10 | 4 | 0.8 |
| Cavalry | 0.65 | 0.25 | 5 | 1.4 |
| Artillery | 0.75 | 0.18 | 8 | 1.0 |

These `TerrainProfile` values live in `TerrainRules`, assigned through `Rules.terrain`. Column multiplies forest slowdown and loss by 0.55. Uphill speed is `1/(1+sensitivity*grade)`. Gentle downhill adds up to 12%; descent beyond 15% grade incurs a sensitivity penalty. Forest and slope speed factors multiply, clamped to 0.2–1.12 before existing fatigue, formation, morale and congestion effects.

Cohesion loss per metre is vegetation loss plus `max(0, slope−0.05)*steep_factor`. It is applied to actual travelled distance, converted to loss/s for debugging. A stationary unit takes no terrain loss. Existing cohesion recovery (including rallying) pauses during active terrain disruption, then resumes; there is no second recovery system and no direct terrain morale drain.

Cavalry and artillery compare a direct route with two short lateral waypoint candidates. Five-to-twenty centre samples estimate forest/slope costs. Only a >15% cost improvement with no more than 40% or 100 m extra travel is accepted; enemy footprints and boundaries remain blocking. One waypoint per stable destination persists at most 120 s, then falls back to the direct, always-traversable route. Friendly avoidance is kept separate and takes priority. Infantry normally accepts direct terrain routes. Routing/withdrawal bypass these detours to keep retreat progress reliable.

### Visibility, fire and morale

Terrain sight rays join the observer and target ground elevations plus 1.7 m eye height. Midpoint sampling uses at most 4 m spacing, reduced to a quarter of the smallest feature radius for narrow obstacles. A surface above the sight line hides the target. Density times horizontal distance accumulates only below a 12 m forest canopy. Vegetation depth of 75 m hides a target; otherwise effective visibility is `exp(-depth/60)`. These queries are deterministic, independent of facing and weapon range. Short-range troops in the same forest can see each other. Fully obscured musket targets are ineligible; partial visibility scales statistical accuracy. A further forest-frontage multiplier (down to 55%) represents difficulty firing an organized volley inside trees. Units at an edge naturally have less vegetation between them than troops deep inside a wood.

Terrain geometry gives high ground sight opportunities rather than a universal shooting damage bonus. Uphill melee power is reduced by `1/(1+2*positive_grade)` between regiment centres. Cavalry charge impact scales down with the greater local forest cover and slope, while gentle downhill movement can help build momentum. Actual movement speed and cohesion remain the main charge inputs. Outnumbering, rally safety and rout-propagation checks also respect terrain perception.

Per-regiment/army perception is distinct from existence in the battle list. Automatic AI and musket target selection exclude terrain-hidden enemies. Infantry can continue to a supplied or last-seen location under an attack/advance order; it does not continually refresh hidden enemy coordinates. Guns can use allied spotting. AI-managed guns stop acquiring/firing at targets with no spotter; live player-directed bombardment can remain blind, retaining the previous artillery-no-direct-LOS design. All unit symbols are still rendered: no graphical fog of war, scouting model, sound detection or search pattern is implemented.

Visibility results are memoized in a bounded per-battle cache keyed by immutable terrain/rules and exact endpoint positions; stationary pairs reuse rays across ticks. Terrain queries do not consume simulation RNG. The camera and renderer cannot change authoritative movement/combat outcomes.

### Cannonballs over terrain

Projectile height is absolute world Z. Launch height is battery ground +1.2 m. Vertical velocity solves the existing fixed horizontal-speed (100 m/s) parabola to the aim point's terrain elevation; downhill shots may initially descend. Scatter increases with the firing battery's forest cover. Direct launch eligibility continues to ignore regimental and terrain LOS, subject to spotting policy for AI.

On nonempty maps, travel is substepped to at most 2 m horizontally (also quarter-feature-radius limited) and 0.025 s. Surface intersections are located by 16 bisection iterations, clipping footprint tests to the first ground contact so a ball cannot damage regiments beyond a ridge through the ground. Contact reflects velocity about the local surface normal with 0.48 normal restitution and 0.78 tangential retention; impact energy retains 72%. The remainder of the tiny collision substep is discarded, a deliberate integrator simplification. Canopy-height forest passages exponentially drain energy (`exp(-0.008*density*distance)`) instead of simulating tree trunks. The existing per-projectile hit set, penetration loss, maximum five bounces/14 s lifetime and friendly-fire accounting remain. Empty Open Field maps retain a single physics step to preserve legacy behaviour.

A high battery can clear a low intervening ridge that a lower battery's trajectory strikes. This is tested geometrically, not granted as a flat artillery bonus. Fire is not guaranteed safe just because a target is visible; the actual shot may hit a ridge, vegetation or a friend.

### Setup, rendering and validation

Automatic artillery packing ranks forest density and slope before preferred grid role; it does not reject usable forest or hill cells. Manual deployment remains restricted only by zones/footprints, not terrain type. AI artillery tries nearby clear-view positions when a ridge obstructs its view, but has no distant-position or ballistic-corridor solver. Hold/defend regiments retain their chosen terrain position.

The renderer caches elevation shading and cosmetic forest graphics plus interpolated 5 m contour segments. Visible portions of the cached surface are cropped before zoom scaling to avoid huge offscreen textures. ## Performance pass and render timing

The measured bottlenecks were repeated visibility sampling, full oriented-rectangle tests for distant units, repeated corner/trigonometric allocation, and rebuilding the same terrain view every frame. The pass makes these changes:

- Automatic fire candidates fail cheap range/arc checks before perception; explicit-target semantics are preserved.
- SAT separation has a conservative bounding-circle broad phase with a small floating-point safety margin. Exact separation remains unchanged for nearby pairs. Bounded LRU caches store direction vectors and immutable corner tuples by pose. Public corner lists are fresh copies, preventing callers from corrupting cached geometry.
- Forest queries reject points outside their bounding box before ellipse maths. Sight rays use scalar coordinates instead of constructing two temporary vectors per sample. Gradient components avoid intermediate vector allocations. The analytic elevation and visibility resolution remain the same, including narrow-feature handling.
- Terrain shading/contours are rasterized into one viewport image and reused until any projection input, map or contour setting changes. This is separate from the world terrain cache and remains cropped before zoom scaling. Smoke buffers are reused, and frames without smoke avoid a full-screen smoke allocation/blit.

`viewer/interpolation.py` holds only previous unit/projectile poses. Before each viewer-driven simulation tick, the previous poses are copied; drawing interpolates toward current state using the remaining fractional timestep. Facing follows the shortest angle. Formation dimensions, strength, morale and combat state stay discrete. Visual interpolation adds up to one simulation tick of presentation latency. Target markers, facing indicators, bars and click hit tests use displayed poses. Terrain/visibility diagnostics still use authoritative simulation coordinates. New balls have no previous pose and snap to current; a bounce also snaps, and displayed height is clamped above terrain. Removed balls are never kept on screen by an old snapshot. Pausing and battle results show exact current state, with lifecycle/reset handling preventing stale poses on resume, restart or deployment.

`Application.update()` uses a configurable `step_budget` of 0.008 real seconds and `max_steps` of four. At least one whole due tick can run, because simulation ticks are not preemptible. After the budget/cap, outstanding simulation time is retained and reported as backlog. Subsequent frames catch up within the same budget; reducing the speed does not erase old debt. This replaces the original performance pass's discarded-debt policy. The HUD reports requested versus achieved speed averaged over approximately half a real second and a persistent recent-window limited indicator. All authoritative simulation time remains tick × 0.1 s; headless execution is unaffected. Inputs replayed at the same simulation ticks still reproduce the same state, even when frame schedules differ.

At the performance revision, the complete suite passed 169 tests. The new performance tests verify conservative broad-phase correctness against polygon intersection, cache safety, target prefiltering, exact sampled visibility equivalence, interpolation/angle wrapping, projectile lifecycle/bounces, read-only rendering, interpolated hit tests, cache invalidation, pause/restart/deployment resets, and budgeted same-tick state equivalence. Timings use injected clocks where necessary; tests do not require a particular machine speed. The benchmark CLI is `python -m analysis.performance`, optionally `--render-frames 300`, `--profile`, `--config`, `--warmup` and `--ticks`.

Before/after checks used seed 17 at 1,000 ticks in both Open Field and Mixed Terrain and matched all regiment fields, projectiles, RNG state, event logs, metrics and result exactly. Representative headless means fell from 2.5→0.75 ms/tick on Open Field and 12.6→7.0–7.4 ms/tick on Mixed Terrain. Warmed initial Mixed Terrain rendering fell from 2.8→1.0 ms/frame (1280 × 800, dummy display). A 180-frame requested-10× Mixed Terrain check held about 62 FPS, achieved 9.15× overall, and had a 14.4 ms p95 update duration. These are environment/workload-specific measurements; the initial terrain build remains around 0.2 s. One costly tick can still overrun the frame budget, and large armies/complex terrain may run below requested speed. Further approximations such as a baked simulation height grid or reduced perception cadence remain future options if profiling warrants them.

### Trait hooks and bounds

- **Prussian Drill:** `d = clamp((cohesion - 40) / 50, 0, 1)`. Reload time multiplies by `1 - 0.25*d`; formation transition time by `1 - 0.4*d`; turn-related and friendly-congestion cohesion loss by `1 - 0.5*d`. Fire accuracy multiplies by `0.85 + 0.30*d`. Ordinary morale shock multiplies by 0.9. Reload/transition bonuses are sampled when that action begins, not recalculated halfway through a timer. Forest slowdown multiplies by 1.25, forest disruption by 1.5 and slope disruption by 1.25. Their high-cohesion benefits disappear under disruption; no terrain or flank immunity exists.
- **Woodland Warfare:** existing forest slowdown/disruption terms multiply by 0.25/0.18, and slope disruption by 0.65. Own-cover firing penalties multiply by 0.25; ordinary range, arcs, terrain occlusion and target-cover transmission still apply. When the target is a woodland specialist, its raw sight-ray vegetation depth multiplies by `1 + 0.55 * target-centre forest density` before the normal visibility and accuracy thresholds. This does not create concealment on open ground or bypass ridges. Baseline accuracy is 1.2× and melee power 0.55×; small strength and loose frontage keep volume low. These represent one woodland detachment's methods, not all forces of its affiliation.
- **Royal Prestige:** effective, steady Grenadiers above 60 morale can support visible friends within 125 m. Each recipient uses at most one anchor, irrespective of count. Non-routing troops receive 0.22 morale/s, rallying troops 0.35 morale/s, and nearby-rout shocks multiply by 0.85. Support sources are snapshotted at the morale pass; there is no recursive emission or self-aura. Routing/shaken/wavering guards cease supporting. Grenadiers start at 96 morale, discipline 1.2, ordinary-shock factor 0.78, melee power 1.5 and safe morale/cohesion recovery 1.25. Casualty percentage still matters, so the smaller regiment remains vulnerable to bombardment and attrition.
- **Exploit the Break:** a Uhlan charge above 3 m/s multiplies its existing charge component by 1.65 when the victim is wavering, routing or below 40 cohesion. Prepared frontal infantry denies this bonus. Existing speed/cohesion/readiness/flank/terrain factors and the 20 s charge cooldown remain; there is no free charge at low speed. Base melee power is only 0.7×, forest disruption 1.25× and slope disruption 1.2×. AI considers visible routing victims, commits after at most 18 s of an initial flank approach, and can withdraw for ten seconds after a damaging contact before a fresh approach. Player-directed attacks remain protected under the existing rules.
- **Aimed Fire:** rifle baseline accuracy is 1.65×. While stationary (speed ≤ 0.1), outside a melee resolution, a further `1 + 0.35 * cohesion_factor * clarity_factor` applies. Cohesion factor ramps from zero at 40 to full at 80; clarity ramps from zero at 0.35 sight transmission to full at 0.9. Moving or heavily obscured rifles lose the extra bonus. Long reload and low strength/frontage limit volume. Forest mobility/disruption factors are 0.8/0.75, weaker than woodland specialists.
- **Rapid Redeployment:** artillery starts deployed. An intended move first marks it limbered and counts down limber time; it cannot fire while limbered or setting up. After stopping, setup time counts down before firing resumes. Changing movement destinations does not bypass either timer. Horse batteries use 1 s / 3 s instead of 3 s / 8 s and launch at 0.65 initial projectile energy instead of 1.0. Existing energy loss, penetration, terrain impact and friendly fire are unchanged. AI horse batteries close into range even when supported infantry exists. Local terrain position searches retain their 60 s commitment/cooldown and moving/setting-up batteries are allowed to finish, avoiding constant replanning.

Modifiers apply once in their existing systems. Probability clamps to [0, 0.95], morale/cohesion to [0, 100], terrain speed retains its existing bounds and friendly congestion remains worst-pair rather than additive. Aura support never stacks by source count. All implemented reload/setup values are positive and all forest/formation modifiers are finite positive constants. Future roster definitions should retain these constraints and the definition tests.

## Add-unit workflow, sustained melee and army strategies

The setup's + Add button opens the existing filtered, detailed roster in transactional batch-add mode. The amount starts at one, is bounded by the per-type cap, and is committed only on confirmation; combined caps include the mirrored addition when symmetry is enabled. Cancel makes no composition changes. Compact army lists show only nonzero archetypes and scroll to an added entry. Existing unit names and the ellipsis button retain inspection/count editing. AI strategy buttons independently cycle blue/red strategy selections even when compositions are symmetric.

Previously melee depended on positive-area intersection while the enemy collision solver removed that intersection immediately. A target could lose damage contact and resume firing, while the attacker steered toward its centre. `geometry.touching()` now uses SAT with a 0.75 m tolerance (and broad-phase rejection). Established Attack/Advance contacts stop movement at the formation edge; melee state is renewed on every contact tick before checking the existing two-second resolution cooldown. That state suppresses ranged fire and identifies the actual contacting target in the HUD. Separation still prevents sustained deep overlap and retreat orders are not latched. Near-destination movement speed is bounded by distance × available turn rate, preventing finite-rate steering from orbiting tight goals.

A second issue was the one-casualty minimum in every melee resolution: it erased the 0.25 artillery melee coefficient at low power. Crew attacks use integer plus seeded fractional rounding of their existing rolled damage, allowing zero casualties on a weak resolution. Crew morale shock is also scaled by 0.25. Organized non-artillery troops retain their casualty floor. Isolated Uhlans versus field artillery at 150 m in seeds 1–5 produced sustained contact and a cavalry win, with 52–68 of 80 riders surviving at that revision; this is a specific regression check, not cavalry immunity to bombardment or prepared infantry. The later broken-unit correction below extends fractional rounding to routing and rallying attackers.

BattleConfig serializes an optional two-entry `ai_strategies` tuple/list using stable IDs `balanced`, `all_out_attack`, `hold_the_line`, `skirmish`; old files default to balanced. Battle snapshots deployment anchors for defensive plans. All-out attack commits infantry/reserves, suppresses automatic light skirmishing and sends cavalry directly in. Hold-the-line keeps troops near initial positions and limits cavalry counterattacks to nearby threats. Skirmish assigns the existing harassment order to ranged infantry. Artillery seeks its normal 85%-range firing distance in all strategies, respecting limber/setup and local terrain-search commitments. Both-hold configurations may intentionally remain in a standoff; no global strategic pathfinder is added.

Cavalry target and waypoint persist throughout an approach. An initial flank leg has an 18 s commitment deadline, with earlier contact/near-target commitment. This replaces the repeatedly recomputed field-edge route and the Uhlan disengagement loop that could abandon a nearly defeated battery. Sustained frontal melee remains a weakness, but AI no longer automatically retreats after its first lightly damaging exchange. Strategy choices apply only to controlled armies, preserve player-targeted attack protection, and are swapped alongside compositions in mirrored headless matchups. ## Broken troops in melee and pursuit

Sustained pursuit exposed the same casualty-floor problem in routing infantry: their old 0.25 damage multiplier was frequently erased by a guaranteed one kill, while their morale shock stayed at full strength. An 80-rider Uhlan regiment therefore lost morale from disproportionate percentage casualties and ordinary melee suppression even when inflicting more absolute losses on 240 fleeing infantry. In seeds 1, 7 and 31, full-strength Uhlans routed after 69.6–71.7 simulated seconds with 50–51 riders remaining.

`Rules.routing_melee_factor` defaults to 0.10 and `rallying_melee_factor` to 0.25. Each scales both outgoing melee power and base morale shock, on top of existing formation, terrain, flank and crew factors. Broken attackers use seeded integer-plus-fractional rounding, permitting zero casualties on an exchange while retaining occasional resistance. Only effective cavalry can deliver a charge; only effective infantry can receive prepared-front protection. Steady/shaken/wavering melee, Uhlan base strength and charge statistics, movement, contact intervals and retreat behaviour are unchanged. Routing alone does not mean invulnerability, guaranteed pursuit success or a free charge at low speed.

At that revision, the same three pursuit seeds overran the routed infantry in 163.9–184.1 simulated seconds, retaining 70–73 of 80 riders and steady morale above 70. Distant reserve regiments prevent army defeat from ending the regression scenario before the actual pursuit resolves; neither reserve participates. Those historical timings are superseded by the distinct pursuit system below. Tests also cover low-strength broken resistance with zero casualties, scaled shock, no broken-cavalry charges, prepared-front restrictions, configurable factors and fixed-seed replays across frame groupings. Terrain, support fire, fresh infantry and prolonged organized melee can still defeat light cavalry.

## Cavalry lifecycle, impact and close pursuit

`simulation/cavalry.py` extends existing Attack/Advance orders and regimental motion. `CavalryState` is approach, charging, melee, pursuit, recoil or reforming; impact is an instantaneous event, not an independent long-lived order. Mutable counters/timers live on the regiment; recipes continue storing archetype IDs and initial orders only. New battles/restarts create fresh state and pursuit schedules. No new dependencies or independent rider entities are introduced.

Ordinary melee power retains strength × 0.013 × (0.3 + cohesion/100), flank, terrain and archetype factors. Steady/shaken/wavering/routing effectiveness is 1/0.7/0.4/0.1, applied once to power and base morale shock. Rallying effectiveness interpolates from 0.25 to 0.60 using the lesser recovery of morale from 28 to 48 and cohesion toward 45. Broken units and crews retain fractional casualty rounding; organized units retain the existing integer minimum. Cavalry sustained power additionally multiplies by 0.8, preserving the Uhlan's 0.7 archetype coefficient. Charges no longer multiply ordinary melee power or retrigger merely because 20 seconds passed.

Charging requires a current live enemy, Attack/Advance intent, distance at most 100 m, at least 12 m clear between projected footprints, heading within 0.55 radians, cohesion at least 50, fatigue below 75, forest cover at most 0.45 and slope at most 0.25. Existing detours must be finished. Normal approach speeds are unchanged. Acceleration is 2 m/s² toward a 12 m/s cap, multiplied by the archetype charge-speed coefficient (Uhlans 1.05); ordinary morale, fatigue, congestion and terrain speed modifiers still apply. Turning uses half normal rate. Movement captures actual boundary-clamped velocity. Charging aborts with poor alignment/cohesion, dense woods, exhaustion or a 16-second timeout; a non-command abort has a three-second retry cooldown. Player orders can cancel immediately.

Momentum is the product of clamped `(actual_speed - 3) / (charge_cap - 3)`, run distance/12 m, cohesion/100, velocity alignment toward the target and forest/slope organization factors from `TerrainRules.charge_*`. It is bounded to [0,1], uses actual movement rather than facing alone, and is reset outside charging; last-impact momentum is recorded separately. A close start cannot instantly obtain high speed or the run-distance factor. Gentle downhill movement can help reach momentum, but no universal elevation damage bonus is added.

An increasing charge sequence is consumed at first enemy contact, including an intervening enemy. Impact power is strength × 0.20 × momentum × flank factor × readiness resistance × the existing Uhlan exploit factor, with ±20% seeded variation and column adjustment. Impact flank/rear factors are 1.5/1.9. Base shock is 24 × momentum with those factors; extra cohesion disruption is 18 × momentum × resistance. The normal damage function still applies casualty morale/cohesion. The charging regiment does not also deliver an ordinary outgoing melee hit on that tick; an organized defender may resist, and subsequent intervals use normal melee.

Readiness exists only for steady, non-transitioning, non-loose infantry. It multiplies a frontal-facing falloff within 60°, stationary readiness (zero at 1.5 m/s) and cohesion readiness from 40 to 90. Impact resistance is `1 - 0.85 * readiness`; high readiness also denies the Uhlan exploit bonus. A held prepared-front impact can cost cavalry cohesion/morale and cause a 40 m recoil goal, followed by four seconds reforming. Movement owns the recoil heading so ordinary target-facing cannot cancel its turn each tick. Escape still depends on normal turning, terrain and physical enemy contact. An unsuccessful escape can leave cavalry in melee; it is not teleported away. Small prepared units can permit recoil/reforming without automatically routing the cavalry.

Recharging requires the original 20-second cooldown, actual clearance of at least 30 m from the last impacted formation, continuous clear reforming time and no recent enemy contact. The spent sequence/last-impact target survive cancellation or repeated orders. Friendly overlap, contact flicker, target state changes and AI replanning cannot renew the impact. Army AI keeps charging/pursuit/recoil/reform commitments; it can select routing targets for all cavalry, penalizes prepared targets in the existing choice, and may recoil from a prolonged disadvantageous engagement. Existing flank approach and explicit player-order protection remain.

Routing enemies use distinct close pursuit when effective cavalry has cohesion at least 25 and fatigue below 85 under Attack/Advance. The contact envelope is a projected-footprint gap of at most 6 m. Pursuers match the fugitive's previous velocity plus available closing space, maintaining about 0.25 m edge clearance instead of freezing for melee or continually pushing the routed rectangle. Turning, physical enemies, terrain and friendly congestion remain authoritative. Multiple pursuers retain the earlier stable approach lanes.

Each target receives one aggregate exchange every two seconds. Contributions are strength × cohesion factor × archetype pursuit coefficient × forest/slope factor. Their sum is divided by sqrt(pursuer count), multiplied by 0.12 and seeded variation, then capped at 12% of the target's original type strength per interval. The integer casualty budget is allocated deterministically among pursuers for attribution. One scattered-resistance budget per fugitive uses its remaining strength × 0.03 × routing effectiveness × cohesion factor, with fractional rounding and very low shock; it is not repeated for each pursuer. Uhlans have a 1.25 pursuit coefficient; their 1.65 Exploit the Break multiplier applies only once to impact, not again to pursuit. Lost contact, resistance/rallying, destroyed/withdrawn targets, exhaustion, disorder and changed orders end pursuit. Organized contact with a different enemy takes precedence.

Remaining simplifications: projected edge distance and previous-tick velocity approximate a close pursuit, not individual interceptions. Breakthrough means collapse followed by pursuit; living enemy rectangles remain physical, with no automatic penetration through a steady formation. Recoil uses a bounded straight local goal rather than an escape pathfinder. Routing now uses local threat-aware escape and permits withdrawal at any outward-reached boundary, as described below. Charges and pursuit have deliberately compressed time/damage, need broader player testing, and are not historically calibrated.

Selected-unit HUD and CH/P/RC/RF symbols expose phases, speed, momentum and last result; impacts have a golden ring. Debug information uses stored state only. New per-regiment analysis counters expose initiations, impacts, momentum, impact losses/shock, failures/aborts, pursuit unit-seconds, inflicted pursuit casualties and pursuit losses. `analysis.cavalry_balance` records elapsed pursuit duration separately from summed unit-seconds, supports fixed seeds and mirrored deployments, and reports results without treating a timeout as a win. Non-routing matchups end when the infantry breaks or cavalry loses effective capability; initially routing matchups continue until destruction, actual withdrawal through any edge or timeout. Distant reserves prevent army victory from truncating the isolated engagement. See `analysis/cavalry_notes.md` and `PERFORMANCE.md` for measured results and methodology.

## Scaling optimization and exact caches

See `PERFORMANCE.md` for the full measured investigation and developer commands. The active benchmark suite spans 6–60 mixed-roster regiments, Open Field/Mixed Terrain, repeated headless timing, normal/debug viewer modes, profiling and paced speed tests. ## Fixed-scale terrain, woodland fighting and bayonet orders

New terrain generation divides fields into sectors up to 1,000 × 600 m and sets feature counts from sector area, instead of stretching geometry. Mixed Terrain counts (hills/forests) are Small 2/2, Medium 5/4, Large 12/12, Huge 27/27, Epic 48/48 and Vast 125/100. Rolling radii remain 70–130 × 72–132 m, height 12–26 m with occasional shallow valleys. Forest radii remain 90–140 × 78–120 m. Central hills and defending-side ridge segments repeat on large maps. The feature cap is now 256 per kind. Terrain RNG remains separate from combat RNG.

`Hill.compact` defaults to false for backward compatibility. Old explicit maps retain Gaussian height `height*exp(-q/2)`; newly generated hills use `height*max(0,1-q/9)^4`, with `q=(dx/rx)^2+(dy/ry)^2`. Their height and gradient smoothly reach zero at three characteristic radii. This permits exact static 200 m spatial cells on maps with more than 16 features; legacy infinite Gaussians/custom subclasses remain global candidates. No Gaussian tails are truncated and no elevation grid approximation is introduced. Indices are excluded from equality/serialization and rebuilt from immutable geometry. Saving preserves the compact flag; old missing flags remain Gaussian. New generation changes maps deliberately, while loading/rematching explicit maps does not regenerate them.

Forest visibility now includes observer and target context in the exact ray-cache key. Woodland observers under full cover multiply vegetation opacity by 0.7; their existing target concealment still multiplies incoming vegetation by up to 1.55. Both taper with local cover. Heights remain geometric and cannot be bypassed. Visible wooded shots use transmission to the power `1-(1-forest_aim)*footprint_cover`; woodland `forest_aim=0.5`, other units default to 1. Their existing own-forest fire penalty remains lower. This raises wooded harassment effectiveness without boosting open-field volleys. A ray stops once vegetation is opaque for that observer: fully hidden vegetation is a lower bound, and the first occluder can be forest rather than a later ridge. Visible-ray sample results remain exact.

The old headcount pressure penalized 140 skirmishers simply for seeing 240 infantry within 160 m, even under safe cover. Loose formations on a Skirmish order now reduce only this outnumbering drain by `1 - 0.9*cover*safe_distance_factor`, bounded to at least 0.1. The factor rises from zero at 25 m to one at 55 m. Open ground and melee retain full pressure; casualty and volley shocks are unchanged. This also applies to other appropriate loose infantry, rather than a unit-name exception. A withdrawal permits up to four seconds of initial turning in addition to its six-second budget, so light infantry can actually move away before re-engaging. Ten-second re-engagement persistence remains. Corners, cavalry and leaving cover still threaten them.

`OrderKind.CHARGE` is an explicit targeted order, available via Ctrl-RMB on an enemy or B for the current target; B also sets an initial deployment order. Cavalry interprets it through its existing lifecycle. Infantry approaches into contact instead of stopping for a musket duel, withholding fire while the order is active. Within 55 m, cohesion at least 35, fatigue below 85, aligned heading and at least 4 m clear run permit an accelerating rush at 1.2× normal formation speed. Acceleration is 0.8 m/s² and turn rate 0.85×; terrain/congestion/fatigue still apply. Movement supplies real velocity and run distance to momentum. Extra fatigue is 0.06/s while rushing.

A first bayonet impact uses strength × 0.035 × momentum × archetype melee × morale effectiveness, with modest flank/terrain effects and seeded variation. Shock is 6 × momentum with flank/terrain effects; additional cohesion damage is 4 × momentum. The ordinary damage system remains authoritative. There is no extra outgoing melee hit on the impact tick. Later contact is normal melee. Infantry uses the existing sequence/impact counters and a 20-second cooldown, plus 18 m clearance and three seconds recovery before another impact. New orders cancel the rush without resetting its spent-impact lock. Artillery charge commands/configurations are rejected. Generic Attack still means closing to musket range and firing; AI does not automatically convert ordinary infantry attacks into rushes.

CH arrows, golden impact rings, target status and selected-unit speed/momentum work for infantry; initial orders serialize as `charge` under the existing recipe format. New fields are live simulation state and restart fresh. Scripted scenarios `bayonets` and `woodland` are available through main.py; their factories can also be imported headlessly. High-ground advantages are verified through existing sight, uphill movement/melee and artillery physics; no new blanket range or damage bonus was added. Known limits are local skirmish steering, modest statistical damage, no perfect retreat planner, and expensive sight checks on densely featured kilometre-scale maps. See PERFORMANCE.md for measurements.

## Independent regiment commanders

`simulation/commanders.py` holds immutable `Commander` identities, stable personality/specialization enums, compatibility tables, fictional name pools, explicit modifier helpers, `CommanderTuning`/`TUNING` and personality `POLICIES`. `Regiment.commander` references that definition independently of `UnitArchetype`; mutable decision deadlines and pursuit anchors live on the regiment. No name checks or officer subclasses appear in combat. Bare `Regiment` objects may have no officer for unit-level controls; ordinary `Battle` construction fills missing officers, and `generate_commanders=False` provides a neutral headless control.

`BattleConfig.prepared()` fills missing assignments using `Random('commanders:<commander_seed>')`, without consuming combat RNG. Names are deduplicated per army where practical and IDs across the battle. The generated 96-bit identifiers, traits and names are serialized on placements. The format remains version 1 with optional new fields, so older scenarios migrate on preparation. Rerolls use a separate seeded stream and saved counter; manual trait changes preserve identity. New-combat-seed rematches retain the stored officers. Restarts construct fresh regiment state. Frozen definitions can be shared safely; a commander cannot be assigned twice in one battle. Background is descriptive and unrestricted. Haudenosaunee commanders use the user-supplied whole-name pool, without invented surnames or European ranks. Original spellings/accents are preserved. Saved service-placeholder assignments remain loadable and unchanged until rerolled.

Trait hooks are situational and intentionally small. Drillmaster scales linearly over cohesion 40–90; it multiplies archetype drill rather than replacing it. Formation duration floors at 50% of normal, manoeuvre disruption at 40%, and combined quiet cohesion recovery at 1.5×. Sharpshooter uses actual sight transmission and cannot improve melee fire; its accuracy combines with rifle Aimed Fire under a 1.5× cap, then the existing final probability clamp. Its 8% reload penalty is unconditional. Artillerist multiplies aim scatter by .88 only while stationary/deployed, and limber/setup by 1.10; it does not change gravity, energy, projectile collision or friendly fire.

Pathfinder reduces existing forest slowdown, slope sensitivity and terrain disruption, rather than bypassing terrain. Firebrand increases only charge morale shock (1.12×) when momentum exceeds .25 and the enemy is exposed; it costs up to three cohesion after impact. It does not add a second impact or additional casualty roll. Pursuit Specialist multiplies the existing bounded pursuit budget; combined archetype/commander pursuit power caps at 1.45×, preserving aggregate multi-pursuer sharing. Its weakness is a .9× frontal power modifier against a steady target. Defensive Tactician requires a stationary Hold/Defend/Reserve order, steady state, cohesion ≥60 and a frontal approach to improve charge resistance and cohesion retention. It gives no universal health/damage reduction; advancing Attack/Advance/Charge orders move 5% slower.

Inspirer reuses the Royal Prestige support query and cadence: 125 m, friendly, visible, steady, morale >60, no self-benefit. A receiver uses only the strongest eligible source: Royal Prestige .22 morale/s (.35 while rallying) or Inspirer .12 (.18). Source count does not add effects, and an Inspirer leading Grenadiers uses the stronger Royal effect alone. Nearby rout shock gets one source multiplier (.85 Royal or .9 Inspirer); commander/archetype resistance has a .6 lower bound for cascades and .65 for direct shock. Full-morale receivers skip unused aura sight queries; rout shocks still query support when needed. Stalwart reduces shock by 10% and adds .04 morale/s while holding shaken/wavering; normal recovery, rout hysteresis and morale bounds remain authoritative.

### Personality decisions and authority

`simulation/commander_ai.py` modifies existing five-second army plans, existing cavalry commitment and per-tick close-pursuit intent. It performs no independent global scan. It consumes already detected enemies. The ordinary spatial, visibility and geometry caches remain in use.

Cavalry commitment multipliers are Aggressive .65, Cautious 1.35, Methodical 1.2, Opportunistic .5 and Stalwart 1.0. Nearby contact opportunities still use existing immediate charge logic. Sustained-melee disengagement thresholds are respectively 18/6/12/12/24 seconds; overmatch thresholds 2.4/1.15/1.65/1.6/2.7. Cautious officers may also pre-empt a locally overwhelming enemy within 90 m. A local disengagement seeks a bounded 40 m retreat, commits ten seconds and cannot repeat for forty seconds. Methodical officers below 55 cohesion may reform for eight seconds, reconsidered on the existing five-second cadence (usually ten seconds of holding); another preparation cannot start for 28 seconds. They therefore still attack at low cohesion instead of waiting forever.

Opportunists score distance, cohesion, vulnerable state and exposed facing among already detected enemies. They keep a living target for ten seconds and thereafter require a 15-point improvement to switch. Aggressive/opportunistic conventional infantry may order a bayonet assault on a vulnerable enemy within 90 m when cohesion is ≥50. Aggressive reserves commit without the ordinary 75-second wait. Stalwart or defensive infantry can hold once inside useful range. Autonomous infantry engagement-distance fractions are .78 Aggressive, .95 Cautious and .85 otherwise. These are preferences, not range/stat changes.

Cavalry autonomous close-pursuit radii, measured from its first close-pursuit position, are 320/120/180/280/140 m in the same personality order. Firebrand increases this by 15%. The limit is a displacement radius, not accumulated path length; it avoids permanently shortening the original approach. Breaking off uses the bounded local retreat and suppresses reacquisition of that target for 30 seconds in army planning. Explicit orders bypass this decision. Per-regiment timers and stable IDs make all decisions deterministic. Charging/pursuing/reforming cavalry retains its existing state commitment.

An additional integration fix protects **all** explicit player orders at army planning, rather than only target attacks. Targeted attacks release that protection when their enemy is gone. A new player order clears commander retreat/reform/pursuit commitments. Existing routing and physical execution limits still override normal commands. A direct Hold is not converted to an autonomous attack, and a cautious officer does not veto an explicit charge.

## Threat-aware flight and multi-gun batteries (2026-11-03)

Routing now uses `simulation/routing.py` through the existing regiment destination calculation. Once per simulated second, it examines effective enemies within 240 m, using existing perception outside 60 m (close physical danger is recognized even through vegetation). Inverse-distance-squared repulsion estimates a preferred escape bearing; eight deterministic headings are evaluated over a 60 m lookahead against weighted danger at the midpoint/endpoint. Paths through known enemy rectangles are discouraged, while an existing overlap allows escape. Weak heading persistence limits oscillation; homeward bias is secondary to immediate safety. No-threat flight retains its previous heading, avoiding a U-turn into the pursuer as it drops beyond sensing range. An initially unthreatened regiment falls back to its army's home direction.

Candidate scoring may look beyond the battlefield to recognize a safe exit; actual movement still clips the oriented footprint to bounds. A routing footprint within 2 m of an edge, with an outward escape bearing, withdraws and emits one event. The normal rally timer, enemy contact, turning, terrain and congestion remain authoritative. Broken troops abandon unfinished formation changes and stale settling goals. Explicit non-routing withdrawal keeps its existing home-edge objective. This is local steering, not full escape pathfinding: fully surrounded regiments may remain trapped, and existing turning/limbering delays can still make escape difficult.

`UnitArchetype.guns` specifies battery capacity (field 6, horse 4). `Regiment.available_guns` is `min(capacity, ceil(capacity * remaining_strength / nominal_strength))` for active artillery, otherwise zero. This is a crew-availability abstraction, not individually tracked damaged guns: one remaining crew strength can support the final gun. It avoids losing a whole gun to the first casualty. Archetype IDs in old recipes automatically use the new definitions; no scenario migration is required.

`projectiles.launch_salvo` launches that many independent balls from positions across 80% of the battery frontage. One battery reload/volley/smoke event covers the salvo. `launch` remains the single-gun ballistic primitive. Scatter is independently sampled for each ball/axis from ±45 m using the battle RNG, multiplied by the existing forest and Artillerist modifiers. It was previously ±18 m. Scatter before the commander factor is capped at half the muzzle-to-target distance at point-blank range, preventing backwards shots. Launch height samples each muzzle's actual elevation. Ball energy, gravity, substeps, bounces, terrain collision, penetration, hit-once guards and friendly fire are unchanged. More balls also mean more physical friendly-fire incidents are possible per salvo. `artillery_shots` counts balls launched separately from battery volleys and is available in batch/report statistics; the HUD reports crew-supported capacity.

Artillery crews retain occasional stochastic resistance without a guaranteed casualty floor. `Rules.artillery_melee_factor=.10` applies to their outgoing melee power and shock (previously .25); `artillery_melee_vulnerability=1.25` multiplies incoming ordinary melee power. It does not increase ranged damage or add another cavalry charge modifier. Firing explicitly rejects non-effective units before reload/effect changes, including batteries called directly in headless tests. These are initial balance settings; six-gun frontal fire is a separate tactical hazard from weak crews in contact.

## Infantry damage, woodland melee and varied artillery pitch (2026-11-03)

`Rules.infantry_volley_damage=1.10` scales the infantry casualty result after statistical hit resolution. `combat.scale_casualties` uses the battle RNG for the fractional part, giving the requested expected increase even when a volley causes only one casualty. Zero losses remain zero; integer/neutral multipliers do not consume an additional rounding roll. Range, accuracy, shot frontage, reload and the fixed .9 volley suppression shock are unchanged. Extra casualties naturally produce the existing casualty morale/cohesion loss. Artillery and cavalry do not receive this volley modifier.

Woodland Skirmishers retain their base .55 melee power, with `UnitArchetype.melee_damage=1.20` and `melee_shock=1.20`. The casualty multiplier applies after the ordinary melee casualty floor/rounding, rather than increasing power and letting integer truncation erase the benefit in one-casualty exchanges. The direct shock multiplier applies separately, so casualty morale loss and direct shock each gain 20% in expectation, rather than multiplying the entire morale result twice. Both hooks also apply to the single bayonet impact. Other archetypes default to 1.0. These bonuses are not forest-only and do not remove the loose-formation, strength or cavalry disadvantages. The roster panel shows the effective melee multiplier; trait descriptions explain the damage/shock bonus.

Each cannonball independently samples `uniform(-3,+3)` m/s vertical launch-speed error after the nominal aim trajectory is calculated (`Rules.artillery_vertical_scatter`). The same deployed Artillerist scatter factor reduces this error. Zero spread disables the extra sample for exact controlled trajectory experiments. Muzzle position/height and horizontal speed remain unchanged: error affects actual flight height, early terrain strikes, overshoot and bouncing. Ground collision is not bypassed, negative launch pitch is permitted and the normal lifetime still bounds every projectile. Added RNG samples intentionally change old battle outcomes; identical current rules/assignments/seeds remain reproducible.

`HAUDENOSAUNEE_NAMES` in `simulation/commanders.py` contains the nine user-supplied names verbatim. Generation selects from unused whole names before falling back to numbered duplicates after pool exhaustion; it adds no surname or European rank. Biography text identifies these as sandbox assignments, without inventing historical actions. Unicode survives commander/configuration JSON round trips. Old saved names, IDs and assignments remain valid and are not silently migrated; deployment rerolls use the new pool. Other cultural name pools are unchanged.

Validation: all **484 tests passed**, covering every infantry archetype's volley multiplier, low-casualty rounding, woodland ordinary/bayonet shock, independent height errors, actual landing variation, projectile termination, supplied names/pool exhaustion/accents and existing simulation regressions. The ridge-clearance geometry test explicitly disables elevation error to isolate its intended comparison. A small five-seed (1–5) control with two standard infantry lines holding 125 m apart, no commanders/terrain/artillery, and a 600 s limit measured mean battle duration **283.1 s at 1.0× versus 266.2 s at 1.1× volley casualties** (about 6% shorter); mean total casualties were 199.2 versus 202.2. This is an isolated pacing check, not a claim about all mixed battles or finished balance.

## Routing cohesion and woodland advantage

`recover_cohesion` is the single shared routing-disorder hook for normal battles and the movement sandbox: routing units lose `Rules.routing_cohesion_loss * dt` (default 1/s), clamp at zero and skip passive recovery. It is not applied again in movement or the routing planner. Terrain, congestion, turning and casualties remain independent disruption sources. Rallying uses the existing recovery rules.

Baseline controlled tests exposed two missing terrain relationships. With seeds 17–21 and side swaps, an ordinary treeline defender lost all 10 equal-infantry firefights; woodland specialists also lost all 10 dense-forest ranged and melee matches. Shared ray attenuation was symmetric, own-forest firing disruption penalized the defender, and ordinary melee had no formation/forest factor.

`combat.forest_fire_factors` now combines existing visibility transmission with outgoing firing disruption, woodland training and incoming protection. The cover fraction is the existing cached five-point formation sample. `depth=min(1, effective_vegetation/40)` controls outgoing formation disruption, so outward treeline shots incur less than deep forest shots. Incoming infantry accuracy is multiplied by `max(.15, 1 - .60 * target_cover * min(1,effective_vegetation/4))`. The target must occupy forest and the ray must pass through vegetation; woodland off the sight line is not free armour. Existing observer/target concealment context contributes to the effective vegetation value. Forest LOS remains an independent eligibility requirement. No extra ray or random detection roll is introduced; artillery bypasses this infantry factor and retains physical canopy energy loss.

The woodland archetype now has `forest_accuracy=3.2`, `forest_melee_damage=4.5` and `forest_melee_shock=2`. These use `TerrainRules.woodland_skill(cover)=clamp(cover/.4,0,1)`: even light woods can support specialist tactics, while bonuses fade continuously at edges. Actual cover, optical depth and movement density are not saturated by this training factor. Base .55 melee power, .65 loose melee frontage, .52 volley frontage, 140 strength and the earlier 1.2 base casualty/shock multipliers remain. The forest melee factor multiplies **strength-dependent power before casualty rounding**, rather than multiplying a guaranteed casualty floor by 4.5; damaged units therefore lose output normally. The small base 20% bonus retains its existing fractional casualty rounding. Bayonet impact uses the same context.

All melee participants use `1 - .45 * forest_cover * formation_density`, bounded below by .2. Density is 1 line, .6 column, .25 loose. This makes conventional masses less effective without pretending every wooded formation is a strong melee defender. Full-cover woodland training doubles direct melee shock in addition to its existing base modifier. Generic archetypes have neutral forest training multipliers. Dense forest therefore favours woodland specialists; ordinary treeline infantry benefits most while keeping a clear outward firing lane.

Woodland covered outnumbering pressure is reduced by up to 90% (the existing 0.1 pressure floor bounds the configured .95 composure), independently of whether the unit is Holding or Skirmishing and including melee. This only reduces the existing headcount penalty, not casualty/volley/rout/flank/charge shock. A nearby effective enemy cavalry unit within 100 m disables the special pressure relief. The existing ranged skirmish composure remains for other eligible units. Skirmish retreat compares woodland fighting capability as well as headcount, samples cover on its first tick, and retains its existing retreat/re-engagement timers. Commander overmatch estimation also reuses forest melee factors at the existing planning cadence, so a cautious commander does not automatically abandon a favourable woodland matchup. Explicit Move/Hold/Attack/Withdraw authority is preserved.

`analysis.woodland_balance` provides mirrored, seeded treeline, stationary wooded ranged, wooded melee, autonomous skirmishing and open-ground controls. Density is configurable; it excludes commanders to isolate terrain/archetype behaviour. Final seeds 17–26, both sides, 450 s limit: dense woodland 20/20 specialist victories for ranged, melee and autonomous skirmishing; light woodland (.45 density) 19/20 ranged, 20/20 melee and 19/20 autonomous victories. Open-ground specialists lost all 20 ranged and all 20 melee controls. Identical ordinary infantry at the treeline won 20/20 dense and 18/20 light cover tests against open opponents. Raw reports are `benchmark-results/woodland-before.json`, `woodland-after.json`, and `woodland-light-after.json`. These are calibrated matchup samples rather than universal win guarantees; positions, starting formation/strength/cohesion, commanders, terrain gaps, range and other attackers can change results.

The ordinary model remains regiment-level, with sampled cover and local AI. There are no individual tree impacts for muskets, no forest editor or new terrain types, and no omniscient woodland vision. Forest borders and very thin patches can provide much less protection than a formation fully occupying cover.

## First roguelike campaign

`Campaign`, `ArmyRegiment` and `Reward` in `campaign/state.py` are immutable records. The five stages use explicit `ready → battle → reward → ready` transitions; defeat/draw terminates the run, and the fifth victory terminates without another reward. Every transition returns a new state. No live Regiment, projectile, AI timer, morale shock or collision state is stored in the persistent army. Starting strength/name were added as optional Placement fields; old recipes default to their previous full-strength/generated-name behaviour. Each deployment creates fresh simulation regiments from a complete recipe.

Persistent regiment IDs, archetype, display name, maximum/current strength and immutable Commander are retained. Post-battle extraction verifies encounter seed, identities, commander assignments, starting strength and non-increasing surviving strength. Routed/withdrawn survivors remain in the roster. Destroyed regiments are removed and their commanders are stored as retired, with no transfer or death mechanics. Total casualties are accumulated before replacements. All fresh battles reset morale/cohesion, fatigue, orders and transient upgrade activation state.

`CampaignRules` sets army cap 10, upgrade cap 3, ordinary recovery 0.30 of missing strength and reward recovery 0.65 of remaining missing strength; both round replacements upward and clamp to maximum. A full army cannot receive recruitment. At-cap or full-strength reward categories are replaced with usable alternatives. If every progression/healing opportunity is exhausted, one Continue option advances normally. Recruits begin fully staffed with new commander identities. Acquired upgrades belong to Commander, independently of archetype, personality and starting specialization. Eligibility restricts class and, where needed, role/formation support. Each upgrade has one rank, no duplicates and no current prerequisites.

Fixed templates use enemy counts 4/5/6/8/11, with one extra infantry in stages 4–5 only when the player has eight or more regiments. The initial army has five regiments: two standard infantry, Woodland Skirmishers, cavalry and artillery. Stages progress from open ground to rolling hills, woodland, mixed terrain and a ridge. Late enemies receive compatible acquired upgrades and useful methodical/opportunistic personalities. These are composition/terrain changes; base unit statistics are not scaled. The first AI balance sample exposed an overly sharp second-stage jump (all three tested upgrade runs lost); it was reduced from six to five enemies, and stage three from seven to six. Autonomous results are diagnostics, not proof of player difficulty or equal reward value.

Deterministic string-labelled Random streams separate encounter/combat seeds, terrain seeds, reward selection and commander recruitment. Generated encounter recipes include explicit terrain, seed and commander data. Pending offers serialize exactly and are validated on load; reopening the reward UI does not reroll them. Successful claim clears pending offers and creates the next fixed encounter. UI autosave writes a temporary JSON and atomically replaces the save **before** changing its in-memory transaction. Failed writes preserve the unclaimed state. Applying results and claiming rewards require their corresponding state, so ordinary repeated clicks/reloads cannot double-claim. Deliberately restoring an external older save is not prevented.

Version-1 campaign saves are distinct from battle configurations and reject malformed data, unknown upgrades, incompatible assignments, duplicate identities, invalid progress, missing fixed seeds, oversized files and mid-combat status. Saving during combat is not implemented: the ready deployment checkpoint remains on disk and Continue restarts that encounter. No migrations between future campaign rules/template revisions are promised; original custom-battle recipes without commander upgrades remain compatible.

`viewer/campaign.py` supplies menu, roster, reward and terminal views. Deployment uses the existing `DeploymentScreen` with composition/officer/file editing disabled; manual placement, facing, formations and orders remain. Back retains a validated deployment. The original `BattleResults` still renders every report/filter/page and receives only an optional Continue action and campaign footer. Custom battles retain restart, new-seed and edit controls; campaigns consume those shortcuts to preserve the run boundary.

Strength/maximum strength are not upgraded. Reload retains a 0.5 s minimum. Swift Reform lowers the combined formation-time floor from 50% to 40% of base; manoeuvre loss retains its existing floor. Quiet cohesion recovery caps at 1.8× with Swift Reform (otherwise the existing 1.5×); aura effects use the single strongest source, never sum Royal Prestige, Inspirer and acquired auras. Combined frontal defence is bounded below at 0.65, direct morale-shock resistance at 0.55 with Steadfast (otherwise 0.65), combined charge shock at 1.8×, prepared artillery scatter at 0.45× and upgraded pursuit power at 1.8×. Existing aggregate pursuit casualty caps and charge-sequence locks remain unchanged. Firebrand disorder only applies to Firebrand, not to all upgraded charge-shock sources.

Rolling support checks run only on actual fire attempts and reuse the firing spatial index. Ambush visibility runs on a volley, inspiration on qualifying hits, and pursuit recovery on an actual rout transition. Stationary preparation is an O(1) timer only for upgraded regiments; unupgraded units allocate no per-tick upgrade state. Morale auras share the existing strongest-source scan. Events use the existing rate-limited trait log; the selected-unit panel lists acquired abilities and preparation/defence requirements.

These rules permit Prussian drill/support builds, Uhlan shock/pursuit chains, woodland ambush/disruption and Grenadier morale anchors without detecting complete named builds. Simplifications: one rank per ability, no commander transfers/experience/deaths, abstract concealment rather than full fog of war, no strategic map, no mid-combat saves, and no reward reroll. A complete campaign playtime and late-stage reward balance still need human playtesting. Tests exercise every mechanic directly, saved reward transactions, legacy recipes and a controlled five-battle run; `analysis.campaign_run` separately measures actual autonomous battles using the existing report format.
